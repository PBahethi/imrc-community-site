Design review · third pass · themes & guidance Tree in the Forest · IMRC community demo 14 September 2026

Six themes, and the one the organisation is named after cannot be selected.

This pass covers what is new since the last review: a six-palette theme system tied to chapters and cities, nine IMRC chapters with leadership and territory maps, and a five-step guided demo tour. The chapter model is the strongest idea in the product so far. The theme layer under it is index-mapped rather than role-mapped, so two of the six palettes put white text on a near-white button and a third misses 4.5:1 — and a label/key mismatch means choosing “Indian Flag” quietly applies the beige Udaipur palette instead.

Credit where it is due

Chapters give the demo something it lacked: a reason the member is somewhere specific. chapter-core.js resolves a chapter from the home city with a sane state-level fallback, the chapter page carries real leadership, committees and initiatives, and the territory map is labelled “schematic… not legal jurisdictions” without being asked. The sidebar is now grouped into What’s happening and Who’s here — exactly the split recommended in the last review — and Votes, Ask the community and Find / offer help finally have navigation entries. The guided tour is the right instinct too: a five-step narrative with one named member is far better onboarding than a feature list.

The theme system, audited

Each palette is a flat list of five hexes. theme.js assigns them by position: colour 1 becomes every primary button, colour 4 becomes eyebrow and secondary-button text, colour 5 becomes the secondary-button and select background. Two of the six palettes (Jodhpur, Udaipur) are tonal ramps of a single light hue, so position 1 is a pale tint and the mapping collapses. Replace five positions with six roles

A palette is not a theme. Define the roles once, let each palette fill them, and a bad combination becomes impossible rather than accidental. Suggested values, keeping each palette’s character and putting every pairing above 4.5:1:

Still open from the second review

The seven new files added since then contain no transition and no @keyframes, and none of the new controls call tip(). So the twelve tooltips and the four motion patterns from the patterns file are unchanged in priority — and the theme work makes them cheaper, because a confirmation chip and a tint both want a role token to colour themselves with.
